**The short answer:** know your practice areas cold, be ready to explain how you track deadlines, and show that you can handle confidential work and demanding attorneys calmly.

Law firm interviews often include a paralegal manager, a senior paralegal, and one or more attorneys. Each listens for something different. The manager wants reliability, the paralegal wants a teammate, and the attorney wants someone who makes their day easier.

## Questions that come up a lot

- What practice areas have you supported, and what tasks did you handle in each?
- How do you calendar and track deadlines? What happens if a date changes?
- Walk me through preparing for a trial, a closing, or a filing.
- Which case management, e-filing, and document review tools have you used?
- Tell me about a time an attorney gave you a rush assignment late in the day.

## Skills to be ready to talk through

Litigation roles may ask about drafting discovery, organizing exhibits, managing productions, and e-filing in the courts you have worked with. Corporate and real estate roles may ask about entity formation, closing checklists, and due diligence. Be specific about what you did yourself versus what you helped with.

Confidentiality and conflicts matter in every firm. Have an example of how you protected sensitive information.

## Bring a writing sample, carefully

Many firms ask for one. Use something you wrote that is public, or a sample you create for the interview. Remove all client names and private details. A clean, short sample beats a long one.

## How to stand out

Accuracy is the job. Mention the checks you use before anything goes to an attorney or the court. Also show you can keep up with volume. Talk about how many matters you supported at once and how you kept them straight.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a paralegal certificate to get interviews?
Many firms prefer one, but experience often counts as much or more. If you have a certificate, list the program. If not, highlight the legal tasks you have handled.

### What if I have only worked in one practice area?
Say so, then point to the skills that transfer, like deadline tracking, drafting, and working with attorneys. Show interest in learning the new area.

### How do I answer questions about a difficult attorney?
Stay professional and specific. Describe how you clarified the request, met the deadline, and kept the relationship steady. Do not criticize anyone by name.

### Should I send a thank-you note to each person I met?
Yes. A short, separate note to each interviewer is standard at most firms, and it shows the attention to detail they want.
