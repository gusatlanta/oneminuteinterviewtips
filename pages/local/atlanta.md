**The short answer:** plan your route like it is part of the interview, leave far more time than the map says, and be ready to talk clearly about commute, hybrid schedules, and which parts of the metro you can work in.

I recruit in Atlanta, and getting to the interview is where a lot of local candidates lose points before they say a word. Here is what helps.

## Plan around traffic

Metro Atlanta traffic is hard to predict. A drive that takes twenty minutes on a Sunday can take much longer on a weekday morning, especially on I-285, GA-400, I-75, and I-85. Check the route at the same time of day a few days before, then add a cushion. Aim to be parked or in the lobby fifteen minutes early. If you are early, wait in the car or a coffee shop nearby.

If a wreck or road closure makes you late, call or text the recruiter or interviewer before the start time. A heads-up goes a long way.

## Consider MARTA

MARTA rail can be a good option for interviews in Midtown, Downtown, Buckhead, and the Perimeter area, since it skips the parking hunt. Check the walk from the station to the building, and leave room for train delays. Many offices in places like Cumberland or Alpharetta are easier to reach by car, so plan for that and look up visitor parking ahead of time.

## Know where you can work

Atlanta jobs are spread across many business areas: Midtown, Buckhead, Downtown, Perimeter, Cumberland, Alpharetta, and more. Before the interview, decide which areas you can reach every day. If the interviewer asks about your commute, answer honestly and with confidence. "I live in Decatur, and I have driven to Perimeter for work before, so I know what to expect" is a strong answer.

## Hybrid and remote questions

Many employers offer hybrid schedules, and the details vary a lot from one company to the next. Ask what the in-office days look like and whether they are fixed. Do not lead with remote work in the first five minutes, but do get a clear answer before an offer.

## Dress and the local market

Office dress around town ranges from business casual to more formal, depending on the industry. When in doubt, go one step dressier than the team dresses every day. Ask the recruiter if you are not sure.

## Frequently asked questions

### How early should I leave for an Atlanta interview?
Check the drive at the same time of day in advance, then add a generous cushion. Plan to be in the lobby about fifteen minutes before your interview.

### Should I mention my commute in the interview?
Only if asked, or when you discuss schedule. Keep it confident and show you have a plan to get there reliably.

### Is it okay to ask about hybrid work in a first interview?
Yes, near the end or when they ask if you have questions. Ask what the schedule looks like rather than asking to be remote.

### What if I am relocating to Atlanta?
Say when you plan to move and which areas you are considering. It helps them see you have thought about the commute.
