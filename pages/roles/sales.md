**The short answer:** know your numbers, explain your process step by step, be ready for a role play, and treat the interview like a sales call where you are the product.

A sales interview is a live test. The hiring manager is watching how you build rapport, ask questions, handle objections, and ask for the next step, because that is what you will do with buyers.

## Know your numbers

Expect "What was your quota, and how did you do against it?" Have the last few years ready: quota, attainment, deal size, sales cycle, and how many accounts or leads you handled. Only share figures you are allowed to share. If you missed a target, say so, explain why, and share what you changed.

## Questions to prepare for

- Walk me through your sales process from the first conversation to close.
- How do you build a pipeline? How much is inbound versus your own prospecting?
- Tell me about a deal you lost and what you learned.
- How do you handle the objection "your price is too high"?
- Which CRM have you used, and how do you keep it current?

## The role play

Many sales interviews include a mock call or the classic "sell me this pen." Do not launch into features. Ask questions first. Who uses it, what they use now, what bothers them about it. Then match one or two benefits to what they told you and ask for the sale. Discovery is what they are grading.

## Close the interview

At the end, ask a closing question. "Is there anything about my background that gives you pause?" Then answer it. Ask about next steps and timing. A sales candidate who does not ask for the next step is missing the easiest test in the room.

Send a follow-up the same day that recaps what you heard and why you fit.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if my numbers were not great last year?
Be honest, give the context in a sentence, and focus on what you did about it, such as building pipeline that closed later or improving your conversion rate.

### How should I prepare for a role play?
Research the company's product and customers, then practice asking discovery questions out loud before you pitch anything.

### Is it pushy to ask for the job at the end?
In sales interviews it is expected. Ask in a friendly way about next steps and whether they have concerns you can address.

### Should I bring a brag book?
A one-page summary of your results and awards can help, as long as it holds no confidential customer information.
