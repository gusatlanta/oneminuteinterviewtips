**The short answer:** show you are organized, discreet, and a step ahead, with clear examples of managing calendars, travel, and competing requests, and be ready for a software skills test.

The best assistants make a busy office feel calm. In the interview, your job is to prove you can keep many moving parts straight without being reminded.

## Questions you will probably hear

- How do you manage a calendar for one person or for several?
- Two executives need the same conference room at the same time. What do you do?
- Tell me about a time you planned a trip or event that hit a snag.
- How do you handle confidential information?
- Which tools do you use every day?

## Skills they may test

Many employers give a short test in Word, Excel, Outlook, or Google Workspace. Practice formatting a document, building a simple spreadsheet with formulas, and managing shared calendars. Typing speed and accuracy may also come up.

For executive assistant roles, expect deeper questions about managing an inbox, screening calls, preparing materials for meetings, and anticipating needs before you are asked.

## Show how you think

Give examples of systems you created. A shared tracker for office supplies. A meeting prep checklist. A travel template that saved your boss time. These show you improve the job, not just do it.

Talk about discretion. You will see private emails, salaries, and sensitive plans. Share how you have protected that trust without giving away anything private.

## What to bring

Your resume, a list of software you know well, and two or three short stories about juggling priorities. If you made a template or tracker that holds no private information, a printed sample can help.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I answer "how do you prioritize"?
Explain how you sort tasks by deadline and impact, check with your manager when two urgent items collide, and keep a running list so nothing drops.

### What if I have not worked as an executive assistant before?
Point to times you supported a busy manager or team, handled scheduling, or kept an office organized. Show you are ready for more responsibility.

### Will there be a skills test?
Often yes. Ask the recruiter or interviewer what it covers so you can practice the right software.

### What should I ask at the end of the interview?
Ask what a typical day looks like and what the last person in the role did especially well. It shows you want to meet their standard.
