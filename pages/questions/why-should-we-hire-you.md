**The short answer:** connect what they need to what you have already done, add one clear example, and end with the value you would add early on.

## Think like the hiring manager

This is your chance to make their decision easy. Before the interview, list the main problems this job exists to solve. You can usually find them in the posting, the company site, and what the interviewer said earlier in the conversation. Then line up your experience against each one.

You are not trying to prove you are better than every other candidate. You do not know them. You are showing that you fit this job well.

## Three moves

**Match.** Name two or three things the role needs, in their words if possible.

**Prove.** Give one short example that shows you have done that work before.

**Look ahead.** Say what you would focus on in your first few months so they can picture you in the seat.

## Example answer

"You mentioned the team is behind on month-end close and moving to a new accounting system. I have done both. At my last company I helped cut our close from about two weeks to eight days by cleaning up our account reconciliations, and I was one of the first people trained when we switched software. In my first ninety days here I would want to learn your current process, find the steps that slow things down, and help the rest of the team get comfortable with the new system."

## Common slips

- A list of adjectives with no evidence.
- Talking only about why you want the job instead of why they need you.
- Running long. Keep it to about a minute and let them dig in.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is this the same as "tell me about yourself"?
No. That question is about your story. This one is a closing argument for why you fit this specific job.

### What if I do not meet every requirement?
Focus on the needs you do meet and show how quickly you have learned new things before. Most postings list more than any one person has.

### Can I mention soft skills?
Yes, but pair them with an example. "I explain numbers in plain English, which is why my manager had me present our results to sales" beats "I am a good communicator."

### Should I bring up something they did not ask about?
If it is a real advantage for this job, yes. This question is a good opening for it.
