**The short answer:** do your homework, give a researched range with your target near the bottom of it, and say you are open to discussing the full offer.

## Prepare before anyone asks

This question can come up in a first phone screen, so have your answer ready early. Look at the posted pay range if the listing has one. Check public salary tools for the same title, level, and area. Talk with people in the field or with a recruiter who places that kind of role. Then decide on three numbers for yourself: your ideal, a fair middle, and your walk-away point.

Keep your walk-away number private. It is for you, not for the interviewer.

## How to say it

Lead with a range, not a single figure. Make the low end a number you would be happy to accept. Mention that benefits, bonus, schedule, and growth all matter to you. This keeps the conversation friendly and gives both sides room.

If you would rather hear their range first, you can ask politely. Some employers will share it and some will not. Either way, have your own answer ready.

## Example answer

"Based on what I have seen for similar roles in this area, I am looking for something between [your low number] and [your high number]. That depends on the full package, like benefits and flexibility, so I am open to talking it through. Can you share the range you have budgeted for this job?"

Fill in the brackets with your own researched numbers. The point is the shape: range, reason, openness, and a question back.

## Things to avoid

- Naming a number with no research behind it.
- Giving a range so wide it means nothing.
- Apologizing for your number or talking yourself down before they react.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if the application form forces a single number?
Enter the low end of your researched range, and add a note if there is a field for it saying you are flexible based on the full package.

### Should I tell them my current salary?
In some states and cities employers are not allowed to ask. Even where they can, you can steer to what you are targeting for this role instead.

### What if their offer comes in below my range?
Thank them, restate your range, and ask if there is room to move on base pay or other parts of the offer, like a review date or extra time off.

### Is it okay to say I am flexible?
Yes, as long as you also give a range. Flexible with no numbers can leave money on the table.
