**The short answer:** share a genuine mistake, take responsibility without excuses, and focus on what you learned and how you work differently now.

## What a good failure story looks like

Interviewers ask behavioral questions like this to see how you handle pressure and setbacks. A strong story has real stakes but did not cause lasting harm. It shows you noticed the problem, owned it, fixed what you could, and changed a habit so it would not happen again.

Use the STAR shape to stay organized: Situation, Task, Action, Result. For a failure question, add one more piece at the end: the lesson.

## Keep the balance right

Spend about a quarter of your time on what went wrong. Spend the rest on what you did next and what you do differently now. If the whole answer is about the mistake, that is what they will remember.

Own your part in plain words. "I missed it" is stronger than "mistakes were made." Do not blame a coworker, even if others played a role.

## Example answer

"Early in my last job I sent a client a proposal with last year's pricing because I reused an old file and did not double-check it. The client caught it before signing, but it was embarrassing and it cost us a day of back and forth. I called the client myself, apologized, and sent the corrected version that afternoon. After that I built a short checklist I run before anything goes out, starting with dates and prices. I have used it on every proposal since, and that kind of error has not happened again."

## Avoid these

- A fake failure that is really a success in disguise.
- A disaster that raises bigger questions about your judgment.
- No lesson. The lesson is the whole point.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use a failure from school or a personal goal?
If you have little work history, yes. Otherwise a work example is stronger because it is closer to the job.

### How recent should the failure be?
Within the last few years is ideal. Very old stories can sound like you are hiding something newer.

### What if the failure was mostly someone else's fault?
Pick a different story. The question is about your part, so choose one where you clearly had a role.

### Is it okay to say the result was still bad?
Yes. Not every story ends well. What matters is what you changed so the next time went better.
