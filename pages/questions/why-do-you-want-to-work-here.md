**The short answer:** name two real, specific things about this company that draw you, and connect each one to the work you want to do.

## Do ten minutes of homework

Generic praise is easy to spot. Before the interview, read the company website, recent news, and the job post again. Look at their social pages and the profiles of people on the team. You are hunting for details: a product they launched, how they serve customers, how they talk about their people, a value they clearly act on.

If you heard about the job from a current employee, that counts too. Say what they told you that caught your attention.

## Two reasons, both specific

One reason should be about the work itself: the kind of problems, the clients, the tools, the pace. The other can be about culture, mission, or growth. Tie each to something true about you.

Leave pay, benefits, and commute out of this answer. They matter, but leading with them suggests any job nearby would do.

## Example answer

"Two things stood out. First, your firm focuses on small and mid-size businesses, and that is where I have done my best work, because I like being close to the owner and seeing the impact. Second, when I spoke with someone on your team, she said new hires get real client work in their first month with a senior person checking it. That is exactly how I learn best."

## What weakens the answer

- Praise that could apply to any company, like "you are a leader in your industry."
- Making it all about what you get.
- Reading the About page back to them word for word.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if I applied to a lot of jobs and do not know much about this one?
Do the research before the interview anyway. Even fifteen minutes on their site gives you enough for a specific answer.

### Can I mention the company's reputation?
Yes, if you are specific about what part of it appeals to you and why.

### Should I say I need a job?
No. Even if it is true, focus on why this one in particular fits you.

### What if it is a staffing agency or recruiter asking?
Talk about the type of role and company you are targeting, so they can match you well.
