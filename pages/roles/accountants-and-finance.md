**The short answer:** expect a mix of technical and judgment questions, bring specific examples from your close and reconciliation work, and practice explaining a number to someone who is not in finance.

I am a CPA as well as a recruiter, so this is the interview I know best. Hiring managers in accounting want to know two things. Can you do the work accurately, and can you be trusted with it when things get busy?

## Questions you are likely to hear

- Walk me through your month-end close. What did you own, and how long did it take?
- How do you handle an account that will not reconcile?
- Tell me about a time you found an error. What did you do?
- Which ERP or accounting systems have you used, and how deeply?
- How do you prioritize when the close, an audit request, and a manager's ad hoc ask all land on the same day?

For senior roles, add forecasting, variance analysis, building a budget, and managing staff.

## Technical topics to review

Brush up on accruals and deferrals, the three financial statements and how they connect, revenue recognition basics, and journal entries for common transactions. If the role touches tax, payroll, or audit, review the areas named in the posting.

Many employers give an Excel test. Be ready for VLOOKUP or XLOOKUP, pivot tables, SUMIFS, and cleaning up messy data. Practice a few times with a timer.

## How to stand out

Talk about controls, not just tasks. Saying you set up a review step that caught duplicate payments shows judgment. Show you can translate. Pick one report you produced and practice explaining what it meant for the business in two sentences.

If you have a CPA, CMA, or are working toward one, say where you are in the process.

## What to bring

A clean copy of your resume, a short list of systems you have used, and two or three examples written out in STAR form. Never bring real financial data from a past employer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to know a specific accounting system to get hired?
Not always. Employers care most that you learn systems quickly. Name the ones you know and describe how fast you picked up the last new one.

### How technical will the interview be?
It depends on the level. Staff roles often include an Excel test and journal entry questions. Manager roles lean more on process, people, and judgment.

### Should I mention I am studying for the CPA exam?
Yes. Share which sections you have passed and your timeline. It shows commitment and helps them plan.

### What is the best question to ask an accounting hiring manager?
Ask what the close looks like today and what they wish were different. The answer tells you where they need help.
