**The short answer:** be ready to talk about IEPs, how you track progress with data, how you work with teachers and families, and how you manage a full caseload across more than one school.

School interviews often happen in front of a panel. You may meet a special education director, a principal, a lead therapist, and sometimes a teacher. Speak to the whole table, not just the person who asked.

## Questions you can expect

- Walk us through how you prepare for and run an IEP meeting.
- How do you write measurable goals and track progress?
- How do you handle a parent who disagrees with the team's recommendation?
- How would you work with a general education teacher to support a student in class?
- How do you manage a large caseload or split time between campuses?
- What experience do you have with behavior plans, assistive technology, or teletherapy?

## Role details to prepare

Speech-language pathologists may be asked about articulation, language, and fluency cases, plus screenings and evaluations. OTs and PTs may get questions about fine motor, sensory, and mobility goals in a school setting. School psychologists should expect questions on evaluations and eligibility. Teachers and paraprofessionals may be asked about classroom routines, de-escalation, and keeping students safe.

Be clear about your license and certification status for the state where you are applying.

## Show your teamwork

Special education is a team effort. Share a story where you, a teacher, and a family worked together and a student made progress. Keep student details private and use no names.

## What to bring

Your resume, license and certification copies, and if you have them, a sample of a de-identified goal or progress report you created. Remove anything that could identify a student.

## Get matched with school jobs

[IEP Staff](https://www.iepstaff.com/) helps school therapists and special education staff find school jobs. You can also browse education openings on [100X Jobs](https://www.100xjobs.com/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I answer questions about a difficult IEP meeting?
Describe how you prepared, listened to the family's concerns, explained the data in plain words, and worked toward a plan the team could agree on.

### What if I have clinic experience but not school experience?
Talk about the skills that carry over, like evaluations and goal writing, and show you understand how schools differ, such as working within the IEP process and the school calendar.

### Should I ask about caseload size?
Yes. Ask how many students are on the caseload and how many schools you would cover. It helps you judge whether the job is doable.

### How do I talk about student progress without breaking privacy?
Share the type of goal and the change you saw, with no names or identifying details.
